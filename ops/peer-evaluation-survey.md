# Peer Evaluation Survey

*Internal spec. Source of truth for the peer form in the Cockpit.*

Sent to everyone on a student's exposure list who is a Foundry member, **when their work
together ends**: at roll-off for client work, or when an internal project is marked done. One
form per person reviewed, per context. The results reach the student only at the midterm and
final releases, combined with at least two other people, and never attributed. See section 4 of
the [developmentship model](developmentship-model.md) for the release rules.

## Why this is not the client form

The [client form](client-feedback.md) and this one look different because the raters are different in three ways
that matter.

| | Client | Peer |
|---|---|---|
| **What they can see** | Two axes clearly, a third sometimes. Never Collaboration | All four, and Collaboration best of all |
| **What they know about the model** | Nothing, deliberately | Everything. They were taught the anchors in week 1 |
| **How they can be wrong** | Too little contact, too polite | Reciprocal inflation, friendship, retaliation |
| **How much we can ask of them** | Five to eight minutes, every 5 weeks | Forty minutes, and they owe it to each other |

So the client form hides the framework and asks what happened. This form shows the framework,
shows the anchors, and spends its design budget on making inflation expensive.

## The four anti-inflation devices

1. **Anchors are printed next to every rating.** Not adjectives. A 5 on Client Hands says
   "the client asks for this person by name," which is hard to hand a friend without noticing
   what you just claimed.
2. **Evidence is required at both extremes.** Any 5 requires a specific instance. Any 1 or 2
   requires a specific instance. Policing only the top would make the form a one-way ratchet
   toward the middle.
3. **The contribution split.** One hundred points between you and the person you are reviewing.
   Zero-sum. The signal is not the number, it is the gap between how you split it and how they
   split it.
4. **Triangulation is visible.** Peer ratings sit next to client and faculty ratings on the same
   axes. Where teammates rate each other high and the client rates them low, the divergence is the
   finding.

**This form never touches money.** Stipends come from logged, verified hours, not from peer
input. If peer ratings moved stipends you would be running two different games on one form, and
the honest one would lose. Say this out loud in week 1.

Scale is **1 to 5**, the published anchors, same as everywhere else.

## Part B. Reviewing each person

*One person, one context. Roughly five minutes.*

*Intro text on the form:*

> Rate against the published anchors, not against the rest of the cohort. There is no curve, so
> rating a teammate honestly costs you nothing. Rate only what you saw. "Could not observe" is a
> real answer and is better than a middling guess.

### The four ratings

Each of the four axes is a 1 to 5 item with the full anchor text printed inline and a
**Could not observe** option.

**Workstream Ownership.** Whether they drive the work.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Waits to be told. Work stops when the next step is unclear. Blockers surface at the deadline | Does assigned tasks well but does not extend past them. Asks what to do next rather than proposing it | Owns a defined piece and finishes it. Raises blockers early. Still needs the plan handed to them | Owns a workstream end to end. Makes the plan, unblocks themselves, reports what they decided | Owns a workstream and pulls others along. Reprioritizes when the client changes direction, and what comes back is better than what was asked for |

**Collaboration.** Whether people want them on their team.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Works alone and the team finds out late. Takes feedback as criticism, or agrees and changes nothing | Does their own part, does not look at anyone else's. Applies feedback only where told | Reliable teammate. Reviews others' work when asked, shares context, acts on feedback next time | Makes the people around them better. Asks for feedback before it is offered, and the correction shows in the next piece of work | Raises the standard of the whole team. Handles disagreement without it becoming personal, and changes position publicly when someone else is right |

**Communication.** The artifacts: updates, decks, memos.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Updates are late or absent. The reader has to ask what happened | Reports activity in the order it happened. The reader extracts the point themselves | Answer first, support underneath. Someone reading the first line knows the state of the work | Writes for the specific reader. Names the decision being asked for. The memo holds up without narration | Produces the artifact the team reuses. A senior stakeholder can act on it without a meeting |

**Client Hands.** Client hands: how they work with the client and earn their trust.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Treats the client as an audience. The client hears about problems or slipped commitments from someone else | Responsive when the client reaches out, but only reacts. Leaves the relationship to a teammate or the advisor | Reliable with the client. Keeps commitments, prepared and credible on their own work. The client does not yet bring them problems | Works with the client as a partner. Understands what they need, says early what will not fit. The client comes to them directly | The client asks for this person by name and brings them problems beyond the scope. Surfaces a risk before the client finds it |

### The evidence questions

**Required if you gave any rating of 5.** Which axis, and what specifically did they do? One
instance is enough. *(Paragraph)*

**Required if you gave any rating of 1 or 2.** Which axis, and what specifically happened?
*(Paragraph)*

**Required of everyone.** What is the one thing this person should work on next semester?
*(Paragraph)*

> Form note: this is the most useful question here. It goes into their development summary close
> to verbatim, unattributed. Write the thing you would say to them directly, in the words you
> would use.

### The principle citation

**Optional, and positive only.** Did you see this person live one of our principles? Which one,
and what did they do?

| Field | Type |
|---|---|
| Principle | Dropdown, the ten from the constitution. Plus "none I can point to" |
| What they did | Paragraph. Required if a principle is selected |

> Form note: this is not a rating and it does not score. Skip it if nothing comes to mind, and
> do not reach for something. **There is no negative version of this question.** You are never
> asked which principle someone failed, here or anywhere else on this form.

Two reasons it is built this way. A principle is not a thing you can rate on a scale, it is a
thing you can point at, so the form asks you to point. And an instrument that collects who
failed a value would be asking twenty-year-olds to judge each other's character for grade
weight, which is not a thing we are going to do. Failures of principle are conduct, they are
raised directly, and they never travel through a survey.

**Required of everyone.** Would you choose this person for your next engagement?

- Yes, first pick
- Yes
- Yes, with reservations
- No
- Not enough contact to say

## Part C. The split

*Intro text on the form:*

> Everyone can rate everyone highly. These two questions cannot be answered that way, which is
> exactly why they are here. They are confidential and never attributed.

1. **Split 100 points between you and this person,** reflecting each person's share of what
   actually got delivered. An even split is a legitimate answer if it is the true one.
2. **Did one of you carry this work?** If so, who, and what would have happened without them?
   *(Paragraph)*

Teams are two people, so the old most-want and least-want questions are gone: with one teammate
they have one possible answer.

## Part D. Yourself

Not here. Your self-ratings live in the [Self-Assessment Survey](self-assessment-survey.md),
which **closes before each release**.

The sequencing is deliberate. Rating yourself in the same sitting where you rate a teammate
anchors your self-rating to theirs, and the calibration gap is only meaningful if the two were
produced independently.

## Part E. Team health

*Intro text on the form:*

> Not shared with your team, and not part of anyone's grade. This is how problems reach Professor
> Murff before they become permanent.

1. Was the load fairly shared? Yes / Mostly / No
2. Did anything go wrong on this engagement that Professor Murff does not know about?
   *(Paragraph)*
3. Did anything happen on this team that made you uncomfortable? *(Paragraph)*
4. What should change about how teams are staffed next semester? *(Paragraph)*

## How the answers are used

| Part | Feeds |
|---|---|
| Exposure list (in the Cockpit) | The reviewer set and the exposure weights. Confirmed against staffing records |
| B, four ratings | The four axis composites, weighted by exposure depth |
| B, evidence questions | Required to validate an extreme rating. A 5 or a 1 with no instance behind it is dropped from the composite |
| B, principle citation | The development summary. Coverage counts across reviewers. Never scored |
| B, "one thing to work on" | The development summary, unattributed |
| B, "next engagement" | Triangulation against the client's version of the same question |
| C1 | Not scored. The gap between the two teammates' splits is a finding, not a number |
| C2, E | Staffing, team design, and conduct. Never a grade input |

**Coverage floor.** An axis with fewer than three independent reviewers is not scored from input
alone. The professor rates it directly and says so in the development summary. Client Hands
on a discovery-only engagement is the case this exists for.
