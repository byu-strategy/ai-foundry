# Client Evaluation Survey

*Internal spec. Source of truth for the Google Form of the same name.*

Run twice per engagement: at **midterm** for development only, and at **end of year** as an
input to grades. Same instrument both times, so the second round is measurable against the
first. Descended from the APM Lab mentor evaluation, rebuilt for a pod delivering to a paying
client rather than a single intern inside a company.

## Design rules

- **The client is not a rater of a framework.** They never see the word "axis," never see the
  diamond, and are never asked to apply an anchor. They are asked what happened. The mapping to
  the four axes happens on our side.
- **Every rating has an escape hatch.** "Not enough contact to say" is on every individual item.
  A blank is worth more than a guess, and the coverage floor depends on honest blanks.
- **The team block always gets answered. The individual block is short on purpose.** A client
  will not write five paragraphs about five students. Two ratings and one sentence each is what
  actually comes back.
- **Behavioral probes beat opinion questions.** "Did anything turn out more optimistic than
  reality" gets an honest answer because it asks about an event. "Rate their integrity" gets a 6.
- **The program section is never shared with students,** which is what makes it honest. It is
  also our renewal and referral data, so it earns its place on commercial grounds alone.

Scale is **1 to 5** everywhere, matching the diamond. The old 1-to-7 is retired: seven levels
against five written anchors forces the rater to interpolate, and interpolation is where
inflation lives.

## Section 1. The team's delivery

*Intro text on the form:*

> These five questions are about the team as a whole, not any individual. Answer from what you
> saw, and use the comment box for anything a number cannot carry. If something was a problem,
> say so plainly. That is more useful to us than a high score.

Each item is a 1 to 5 rating with an optional comment box beneath it. The form asks for a
comment on anything rated 3 or below.

**1. Timeliness.** Did the team deliver what they promised, when they promised it?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deadlines slipped without warning | Frequently late, usually flagged | Mostly on time | Consistently on time, slippage flagged early | Ahead of us. We never had to ask where something was |

**2. Communication.** Could you tell where the work stood without chasing anyone?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| We were in the dark | We found out when we asked | Regular updates we could follow | Updates we could act on without a meeting | We forwarded their updates to other people internally |

**3. Agency.** Did the team move the work forward on its own?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Waited to be told at every step | Did what was asked, nothing more | Owned their piece once scoped | Proposed the next step before we did | Reframed the problem in a way that changed what we asked for |

**4. Technical competency.** Did they build something that actually works?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Did not work | Worked in a demo, not in our environment | Works, with known gaps | Works in our environment, and we understand how | Our own people can run and extend it without them |

**5. Contribution.** Was the output worth the engagement?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| We would not do this again | Below what we expected | About what we expected | Better than we expected | It changed a decision we were going to make anyway |

**Required.** What is the single thing that would have made this engagement better?
*(Paragraph)*

## Section 2. The individuals

*Intro text on the form:*

> This block repeats for each student on your team. Rate only what you saw directly. "Not enough
> contact to say" is a real and useful answer, and we would much rather have it than a guess.

Repeat per student. Four questions, roughly two minutes each.

**A. In front of you.** How did this person show up in meetings and on calls?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Silent, or created confusion someone else fixed | Answered direct questions, arrived unprepared | Prepared and credible on their own work | Ran part of the meeting, said plainly what would not fit | We would ask for this person by name |

*Plus:* Not enough contact to say

**B. What they sent you.** Could you act on their written work without them in the room?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Had to ask what it meant | Had to dig for the point | Clear, we knew where things stood | Written for us specifically, named the decision we had to make | We reused it internally |

*Plus:* Not enough contact to say

**C. Ownership.** *(Optional. Answer only if you saw it.)* Did they drive their piece of the
work, or wait to be pointed at it?

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Waited | Did assigned tasks | Owned a defined piece | Owned it end to end and told us what they decided | Owned it and pulled others along |

*Plus:* Not enough contact to say

**D. Required, one sentence each.** One thing this person did well. One thing they should work on.
*(Paragraph)*

**E.** Would you want this person on your next engagement?

- Yes, specifically
- Yes
- Yes, with reservations
- No
- Not enough contact to say

## Section 3. What actually happened

*Intro text on the form:*

> Four short questions about specific moments. These tell us more than any rating does.

1. **Did anything the team told you turn out to be more optimistic than reality?** What was it,
   and how did you find out? *(Paragraph)*
2. **When you needed something, did you go to a particular person or to the group?** Who, and
   why them? *(Paragraph)*
3. **Was there a moment someone told you something you did not want to hear?** Who, and what
   happened after? *(Paragraph)*
4. **Did anyone on the team surprise you, in either direction?** *(Paragraph)*
5. **Did anyone on the team do something that stuck with you?** It does not have to be about the
   deliverable. *(Paragraph)*

Question 5 is our principle citation, asked in plain language. Clients do not know our
constitution and are never shown it, so they are asked for the moment and we do the tagging on
our side. It is positive-only by construction and there is no negative counterpart anywhere on
this form.

Question 1 is the most valuable item on the form. It asks about an event rather than asking a
client to criticize a student, which is why it gets an honest answer. Question 2 identifies the
person the client actually trusts, without asking anyone to rank anybody.

## Section 4. Program feedback

*Intro text on the form:*

> This section is not shared with the students. It is for us, to decide whether the program is
> worth running the way we are running it.

1. **Value against fee.** Set against what you paid, how would you characterize the return?
   - Well below the fee
   - Somewhat below the fee
   - About equal to the fee
   - Somewhat above the fee
   - Well above the fee

2. **Your own investment.** How much of your team's time did supervising this engagement take?
   - Far more than we expected
   - Somewhat more than we expected
   - About what we expected
   - Less than we expected

3. **Team size.** The pod was two dedicated builders plus an engagement lead. For work like
   yours, what would have been right?
   - Fewer people
   - The same
   - One more builder
   - Substantially larger

4. **Would you engage the Foundry again?**
   - Yes, already planning it
   - Yes, likely
   - Maybe, depends on the scope
   - No

5. **Would you refer us to another company?** Yes / Maybe / No. If yes, who should we talk to?

6. **If you were assigning a letter grade to this team's overall performance, what would it be?**
   A / A- / B+ / B / B- / C+ or lower
   *(Form note: Professor Murff sets final grades from several inputs and welcomes yours.)*

7. **Anything else about the engagement, the fee, or the program that would help us improve it?**
   *(Paragraph)*

## How the answers are used

| Form section | Feeds |
|---|---|
| Section 1 | Client Evaluation component, team level, same score for everyone on the pod |
| Section 2A | Client Presence axis, client weighted Deep |
| Section 2B | Communication axis, client weighted Deep |
| Section 2C | Workstream Ownership axis, client weighted Moderate, skipped if blank |
| Section 2D | Development summary, quoted unattributed |
| Section 2E | Triangulation. A "no" against high peer ratings is a flag worth a conversation |
| Section 3, Q1 to Q4 | Evidence the professor rates against when sources disagree. Not scored directly |
| Section 3, Q5 | Tagged to a principle on our side. Development summary only. Never scored |
| Section 4 | Never enters a grade. Program, pricing, and renewal decisions |

Section 2 gives no rating on Collaboration. That axis is invisible to a client and comes from
peers and faculty only.
