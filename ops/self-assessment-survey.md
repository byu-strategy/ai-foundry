# Self-Assessment Survey

*Internal spec. Source of truth for the Google Form of the same name.*

The third of the three instruments. Run at the **same two moments** as the other two, midterm and
end of year, and **submitted before you see anyone else's input**. That ordering is the whole
point: a self-rating collected after you have read your reviews measures nothing.

## What this is for

Your self-assessment is **never in your composite** and is **never graded as a rating**. It does
two things:

1. **It produces your calibration gap.** Your self composite minus your actual composite. A
   persistent gap in either direction is a finding worth a conversation. Overrating is the more
   common one and the more useful to catch. Underrating is not humility, it is a broken
   instrument for staffing yourself.
2. **It is the input to your video reflection,** which *is* graded, on how honestly you engage
   with the distance between the two.

This is the rule that holds the whole model together: **demonstrated proficiency is graded and a
self-rating never is.** The moment a self-report carries rating weight it stops measuring
anything, and it would reward understating your baseline.

## Why it is a separate form and not a section of the peer form

Three reasons, and they are all about sequencing.

- **It must close before peer input opens.** If you rate yourself in the same sitting where you
  rate five teammates, your ratings of them anchor your rating of yourself.
- **It asks different questions.** The peer and client forms ask what somebody *observed*. This
  one asks you to *produce evidence*, which is a harder task and the only version of self-rating
  that resists wishful thinking.
- **It is the one instrument you keep.** Your own four numbers across four rounds are the
  clearest picture anyone in the Foundry gets of their own trajectory.

Scale is **1 to 5**, the published anchors, identical to the other two forms.

## Section 1. Your four ratings

*Intro text on the form:*

> Rate yourself against the same anchors everyone else is using. They are printed below each
> question. Nobody's grade moves because of what you put here, in either direction. What moves is
> how well you know yourself, which is measured later, by comparing this to what came back.

Four items, 1 to 5, full anchor text printed inline, identical wording to the peer form:

- **Workstream Ownership**
- **Collaboration**
- **Communication**
- **Client Presence**

**No "could not observe" option.** You were there for all of it.

---

## Section 2. Your evidence

*Intro text on the form:*

> One paragraph each. Point at something that happened. If you cannot name an instance, that is
> the most useful thing this form will tell you, and the honest answer is to say so.

Each question is the same underlying question the other two forms ask, turned around to face you.

| # | Question | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What did you own end to end this semester** that nobody had to chase you about? Who decided what the steps were? | Workstream Ownership |
| 2 | **When did the work get stuck, and what did you do next?** How did the rest of the pod find out, and when? | Workstream Ownership |
| 3 | **What did you do that made someone else's work better?** Name the person and the piece of work. | Collaboration |
| 4 | **What is the last correction you received, and what changed in the next piece of work?** | Collaboration |
| 5 | **Point to one thing you wrote that someone acted on** without needing you in the room. | Communication |
| 6 | **When something was going badly, how did the client and the pod hear about it?** From you, from someone else, or from the result? | Communication |
| 7 | **Describe a moment you owned in front of the client.** What did you actually say? | Client Presence |
| 8 | **Was there something the client needed to hear that they would not like?** Who said it? | Client Presence |

**9. Which principle did you live this semester, and what did you do?** Pick one from the
constitution and point at something specific. *(Dropdown plus paragraph. Positive only. You are
not asked which principle you failed, and neither is anyone else.)*

Questions 2, 6 and 8 are the ones that produce honest answers, because they ask about a specific
event with a specific outcome rather than asking you to characterize yourself.

## Section 3. Prediction

*Intro text on the form:*

> Before you see any of it. This is the only part of the form where being right counts for
> anything, and it counts in your video reflection, not in your ratings.

1. **On which axis do you expect your reviewers to rate you highest?** Why? *(Paragraph)*
2. **On which axis do you expect them to rate you lowest?** Why? *(Paragraph)*
3. **What is the one criticism you expect to see,** stated the way you think they will state it?
   *(Paragraph)*
4. **Who on your pod do you think would pick you first for their next engagement, and who would
   not?** *(Paragraph. Never shared with anyone named.)*

Question 3 is the highest-value item on this form. A student who can write their own criticism
before reading it has already done the developmental work, and a student who cannot has told us
where to spend the conversation.

---

## Section 4. Forward

1. **What is the one thing you are working on next semester?** *(Paragraph)*
2. **What would have to be true for you to move one full point on your lowest axis?**
   *(Paragraph)*
3. **What do you need from the Foundry that you are not getting?** *(Paragraph. Read by faculty
   only.)*

## How the answers are used

| Section | Feeds |
|---|---|
| 1 | The calibration gap. Compared against your composite, never averaged into it |
| 2, Q9 | Compared against which principles your reviewers cited for you. A principle you claim that nobody else saw is worth a conversation, never a penalty |
| 2, Q1 to Q8 | Read alongside your reviewers' evidence. Where your account of an event and theirs diverge materially, that is an interview trigger |
| 3 | The video reflection prompt, and the development summary's opening line |
| 4.1 and 4.2 | Your development summary and next semester's staffing |
| 4.3 | Program improvement. Never a grade input, never attributed |

### The calibration gap

| Gap (self minus actual) | Reading |
|---|---|
| +1.0 or more | Overrating. Named directly in the development summary and in the midterm conversation |
| +0.3 to +0.9 | Normal. Almost everyone rates themselves slightly high |
| -0.3 to +0.3 | Calibrated |
| -0.4 or lower | Underrating. Worth a conversation, because it usually shows up as not volunteering for work you can do |

The gap is **reported, never scored.** What is scored is whether your video reflection engages
with it honestly.
