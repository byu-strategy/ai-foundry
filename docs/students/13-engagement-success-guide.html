Engagement Success Guide

Your client has the same AI tools you do. They hire the Foundry for people who read what they were given, decide what matters, do the work, and say what they recommend. If what you bring to a call is something the client could have produced by prompting a model, you have added nothing they could not get without you.

This page is short on purpose. Read it before your first client call, and run the checklist every week.

Your team

Think of the team as a small consulting firm: the Project Advisor is the partner, the Senior Builder-Consultant is the engagement manager, and the Builder-Consultant is the associate. One difference from a traditional firm: with AI, both students build and consult. The senior role adds coordination; it does not take anyone away from the work.

Role Who Like a What they own
Project Advisor Professor Murff or Professor Toborg Partner The client relationship: finding the client, the scope and the fee. The standard of the work and coaching
Senior Builder-Consultant A second-year MBA Engagement manager Everything a Builder-Consultant does, plus coordination: the client’s main day-to-day contact, the final say on sprint priorities, and a last review before work goes out
Builder-Consultant An undergraduate junior or senior Associate Builds and consults: owns workstreams end to end, talks with the client, and presents their own work

Both Builder-Consultants:

  • Build and ship: each owns workstreams end to end, from build to demo
  • Consult: talk with the client directly, bring a recommendation to every meeting, and present your own work
  • Read the client’s code, documents and data before asking about them
  • Deliver what was agreed, on the date agreed, and reply to the client the same day
  • Plan each sprint together and demo every two weeks
  • Raise risks to your Project Advisor the same day
  • Give each other feedback every two weeks

The Senior Builder-Consultant also:

  • Is the client’s main day-to-day contact
  • Has the final say on sprint priorities when the two of you disagree
  • Gives work a last review before the client sees it

The Project Advisor:

  • Owns the client relationship: finds the client and sets the scope and fee
  • Answers to the client for the result
  • Runs the Thursday sprint review and coaches you both
  • Steps in when something is at risk

Who answers for what. The Project Advisor answers to the client. The Senior Builder-Consultant answers to the Project Advisor for the result. Each Builder-Consultant answers for their own work.

Earning the lead. In a consulting firm, an engagement manager has usually been an associate first. Most Senior Builder-Consultants have not, so the lead is handed over in stages. At the start, your Project Advisor leads client meetings with the Senior Builder-Consultant beside them. Once the Senior Builder-Consultant has shown they can, they lead the meetings, and the Project Advisor joins the important ones.

Everyone on the team commits 20 hours a week.

Two projects

Every team works on two projects at once: an outside client engagement and a Foundry product. Both have stakeholders who depend on you, and the Foundry products will have customers of their own. Treat both stakeholders as clients: everything on this page applies to each of them.

  • Plan every sprint across both projects, with dates for each
  • Agree with each stakeholder on what they get and when
  • When one project needs more of your week, tell the other project’s stakeholder what moves, before it moves
  • Never explain a missed date to one stakeholder by pointing at the other project
  • Build the Foundry products against made-up data, never real alumni or student records

What the client expects

These come from what clients told us when an engagement went badly.

The client expects What that means in practice
The hours you committed Work them every week. If you cannot, tell the client and faculty before the week starts.
Contact between calls Reply to every client message the day it arrives, even if only to acknowledge it and say when the full answer is coming. A message that arrives after 10 PM gets its reply the next day. Send at least one short written update between weekly calls. Ask questions during working hours, as they come up.
What they asked for Confirm each requested deliverable in writing the same day, and deliver it by the date. If you think something else would serve them better, deliver what they asked for first, then propose the alternative.
Homework done Read the code, documents, and data the client gave you before asking about them. Keep a running log of their answers, and never ask the same question twice.
A point of view Bring a recommendation and your reasoning to every call. Questions are for what you could not find out yourself.
Work you stand behind Use AI for the work. Check everything it produces, and give the client only what you have verified and can explain line by line. Never send unreviewed AI output or ask the client to check it for you.

The first week

The first week decides how the rest goes. Most stalled engagements stalled here, waiting on access nobody asked for.

Day one: ask for access, in one message. Send the client a single list of everything you need, with who on the team needs each item:

  • Code repositories, and the hosting and deployment accounts behind them
  • Data: exports, databases, or read-only reports, and who owns each
  • Documents: process maps, past attempts, and anything the client has already written down
  • The tools the team will use with them: Slack or Teams channel, shared drive, project board
  • Test accounts for any system you will build against
  • Names of the people you should talk to, beyond your main contact

Follow up on anything missing after two business days. Access still blocked after that is a blocker: raise it at the deep dive and with your Project Advisor.

Before you touch client data, sign any nondisclosure agreement the client asks for, and read the data and confidentiality terms of the engagement letter. Some clients bar public AI tools or require the Foundry’s own accounts. Never put client data in a personal account.

The kickoff call. Come with an agenda, and leave with these agreed in writing:

  • What success looks like at the end of the engagement, in the client’s words
  • Who decides, who your day-to-day contact is, and who else needs to be kept informed
  • How you will communicate, and when the weekly call is
  • The first deliverable and its date

Send the summary the same day.

The Consultant diamond on a client team

Your clients and colleagues rate you on the four axes of the Consultant diamond (The Developmentship Model). On an engagement, each axis asks one question.

Axis The question On a client team
Judgment Is it the right work? Decide what matters most to the client. Form a hypothesis and test it against their data and materials.
Ownership Does the work move? Own the result from the client’s question to working software. Nobody has to chase you.
Communication Can the client act on it without you in the room? Lead with the answer. Updates say what was done, what is next, and what you need from them.
Collaboration Does the client trust you and want to keep working with you? Be on time and prepared. Take pushback well. Carry your share inside the team.

Ownership

You own the outcome for the client, from the question to the working result. The constitution calls this high agency: you see the gap and close it.

Ownership looks like:

  • Anticipating what the client needs before they ask
  • Flagging a risk or a slip as soon as you see it
  • Delivering complete work, ready to use
  • Following up on every open item until it closes

Ownership does not look like:

  • Waiting for instructions
  • Handing over a draft and asking the client to finish it
  • Explaining afterward why something was not yours

When to bring in your Project Advisor

Tell your Project Advisor the same day when:

  • The client asks for work outside the engagement letter. Do not agree to it or refuse it; say you will confirm and come back to them
  • The client has not answered a question that blocks you for three business days
  • Access is still blocked after two business days of follow-up
  • You will miss a date you committed to
  • A teammate is not carrying their share
  • Anything you would not want to read about in the news, including a possible confidentiality slip

Raising it early is part of the job. Raising it late is what costs the client.

How your work is checked

Every engagement is checked weekly by faculty and by the client at set points. None of these is a surprise, and each one is a chance to fix a problem while there is time.

Check When Who What happens
Faculty deep dive Weekly, 25 minutes, Thursday class Your team with your Project Advisor You walk through the deep dive agenda
Client midpoint check-in Week 3 of the engagement The client’s sponsor, about one minute Rates no one. A low score means faculty talks with you and the client that week
Client team review End of each block of the engagement Every client contact who worked with you Rates each student. Feeds your colleague and client feedback grade
Peer evaluation When your work together ends Every Foundry member who worked with you Rates each student. Feeds your colleague and client feedback grade
Self-assessment Before the midterm and final releases You Never part of your score. Compared with what others saw
Sprint review and demo Every two weeks You Graded as part of the sprint (Sprints)

A client can write to Professor Murff directly at any time. When a client raises a concern, faculty talks with your team that week.

The weekly deep dive

Twenty-five minutes, three questions, asked about both projects. Bring the actual work, with no slides.

  1. What did each stakeholder ask for last week, and what did you deliver? Show the artifact.
  2. What will you recommend to each stakeholder this week, and why?
  3. What are you stuck on? Anything you held for more than 48 hours gets named here.

On LinkedIn and your resume

Use your Foundry role as your title. The organization is AI Foundry, BYU Marriott School of Business.

Your role Titles you can use
Senior Builder-Consultant Senior AI Builder · Lead Forward-Deployed Engineer · Senior Builder-Consultant
Builder-Consultant AI Builder · Forward-Deployed Engineer · Builder-Consultant

Describe what you did and for what kind of client. Name the client only if your engagement letter allows it; otherwise describe the client by industry and size. For example:

Built and shipped an intake system for a regional health clinic, cutting incomplete intakes from 39% to 17% in two weeks. Led discovery with the clinic’s operations team and handed the system over to their staff.

Keep it accurate. The Foundry is a for-credit program, so do not list it as a paid job or an internship. Leave LinkedIn’s employment type blank. Do not post screenshots, code, data, or client names that the engagement letter keeps confidential, and ask your Project Advisor when you are not sure.

Your other commitments

Your application listed your other courses, work, projects, and absences. Keep it current: update it within a week of any change, and tell your team before a planned absence. A change you tell us about early is something we can plan around.
