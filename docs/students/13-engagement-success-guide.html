Engagement Success Guide

Clients hire the Foundry with high expectations. This guide exists to help teams meet and exceed those expectations, from the first week to roll-off.

Clients have the same AI tools we do. They are looking for expertise, critical thinking and good judgment beyond what they get from AI tools and coding agents alone. Read this guide before your first client call, and refer to the checklist often.

Your team

Each client team has three people: a Faculty Project Advisor (Professor Murff or Professor Toborg), an MBA Engagement Lead and an undergraduate Associate. Top-tier consulting firms staff engagements this way because the structure supports coaching, mentorship and growth at every level. To deliver excellent work consistently, the Foundry operates the same way.

Your Project Advisor works closely with your team: solving problems with you and reviewing deliverables before they reach the client. They meet with each team for 25 minutes in the Thursday 9:30 to 10:45 slot, meet at other times as needed, and frequently join client calls.

Role Who What they own
Faculty Project Advisor Professor Murff or Professor Toborg The client relationship: finding the client, the scope and the fee. Answers to the client for the result. Works through problems with the team, reviews deliverables, meets with the team every Thursday, and joins client calls
Engagement Lead A second-year MBA Everything an Associate does, plus: the client’s main day-to-day contact, the final say on sprint priorities when the two of you disagree, and a review of the team’s work before it goes to the Project Advisor and the client. Answers to the Project Advisor for the result
Associate An undergraduate junior or senior Owns workstreams end to end, talks with the client, and presents their own work

Because AI lets everyone build, both students build and consult. The Engagement Lead’s coordination is added to their build work.

Both of you:

  • Build and ship your workstreams, from build to demo
  • Talk with the client directly, bring a point of view to every meeting, and present your own work
  • Read the client’s code, documents and data before asking about them
  • Deliver what was agreed, on the date agreed, and reply to the client the same day
  • Plan each sprint together and demo every week
  • Raise risks to your Project Advisor the same day
  • Give each other feedback every two weeks

Leading client engagements. In a consulting firm, the engagement lead has typically been an associate first. Most Engagement Leads in the Foundry have not, so taking the lead may be a gradual transition from the Project Advisor. Early on, your Project Advisor may lead client meetings with the Engagement Lead alongside. As the Engagement Lead demonstrates they are ready, they begin to lead meetings, and the Project Advisor continues to join calls where helpful.

Both the Engagement Lead and the Associate spend 20 hours a week on the team’s work.

Two projects

Every team runs two projects at once: an outside client engagement and a Foundry product. Each has stakeholders who depend on you, and the Foundry products will have customers of their own. Treat both stakeholders as clients; everything on this page applies to each.

There are three Foundry products, and two teams work on each one. Client work rises and falls from week to week, so when one team’s client needs more of its time, the other team steps up.

What clients expect

Clients who hire the AI Foundry expect professional-level work.

Clients expect What it looks like
The hours you committed 20 hours a week, the commitment for 6.0 credit hours.
Contact between calls Reply to every client message the day it arrives, ideally within minutes or hours, even if only to acknowledge it and say when the full answer is coming. If a message comes in after 10 PM, a reply the following day is appropriate. In general, overcommunicate, so the client always knows where things stand and what to expect from you next.
You’ve done your homework Read the code, documents and data the client gave you before asking questions whose answers may be in them. Keep a running log of their answers so each question is asked once.
Your own thinking AI gives everyone a starting point. Clients hire you for your independent perspective on top of it: what matters most for them, the tradeoffs, and a view you can defend. When a request is ambiguous, work out what they most likely need, state your assumptions, and keep moving. Always have a point of view on the right answer. Update it as new facts emerge, be ready to share it, and save your questions for what the materials you have cannot answer.
A human touch Use AI for the work, then make it yours: read, check and edit everything before it goes out, emails included. Never send a deliverable, email or other output you cannot stand behind and explain line by line.

The first week

The first week sets the pace for the engagement. Access is the most common early delay, so ask for it on day one.

Day one: ask for access, in one message. Send the client a single list of everything you need, with who on the team needs each item:

  • Code repositories, and the hosting and deployment accounts behind them
  • Data: exports, databases, or read-only reports, and who owns each
  • Documents: process maps, past attempts, and anything the client has already written down
  • The tools the team will use with them: Slack or Teams channel, shared drive, project board
  • Test accounts for any system you will build against
  • Names of the people you should talk to, beyond your main contact

Follow up on anything missing after two business days. If access is still blocked after that, raise it at the deep dive and with your Project Advisor.

Before you touch client data, sign any nondisclosure agreement the client asks for, and read the data and confidentiality terms of the engagement letter. Some clients bar public AI tools or require the Foundry’s own accounts. Keep client data in Foundry or client accounts, never personal ones.

The kickoff call. Come with an agenda, and leave with these agreed in writing:

  • What success looks like at the end of the engagement, in the client’s words
  • Who decides, who your day-to-day contact is, and who else needs to be kept informed
  • How you will communicate, and when the weekly call is
  • The first deliverable and its date

Send the summary the same day.

The Consultant diamond on a client team

Your clients and colleagues rate you on the four axes of the Consultant diamond (The Developmentship Model). On an engagement, each axis asks one question.

Axis The question On a client team
Judgment Is it the right work? Decide what matters most to the client. Form a hypothesis and test it against their data and materials.
Ownership Does the work move? Own the result from the client’s question to working software, without needing reminders.
Communication Can the client act on it without you in the room? Lead with the answer. Updates say what was done, what is next, and what you need from them.
Collaboration Does the client trust you and want to keep working with you? Be on time and prepared. Take pushback well. Carry your share inside the team.

Ownership

You own the outcome for the client, from the question to the working result. The constitution calls this high agency: you see the gap and close it. In practice that means:

  • Anticipating what the client needs before they ask
  • Flagging a risk or a slip as soon as you see it
  • Delivering complete work, ready to use
  • Following up on every open item until it closes

When to bring in your Project Advisor

Tell your Project Advisor the same day when:

  • The client asks for work outside the engagement letter. Tell the client you will confirm and come back to them, then check with your Project Advisor before agreeing or declining
  • The client has not answered a question that blocks you for three business days
  • Access is still blocked after two business days of follow-up
  • You will miss a date you committed to
  • A teammate is not carrying their share
  • Anything you would not want to read about in the news, including a possible confidentiality slip

Raise it the day you see it, while there is still time to fix it.

How your work is checked

Faculty and clients check in on every engagement at set times, so you hear how it is going while there is time to adjust.

Check When Who What happens
Faculty deep dive Weekly, 25 minutes, in the Thursday 9:30 to 10:45 slot Your team with your Project Advisor You walk through the deep dive agenda
Client midpoint check-in Week 3 of the engagement The client’s sponsor, about one minute Rates the engagement as a whole. If the score is low, faculty meets with you and the client that week
Client team review End of each block of the engagement Every client contact who worked with you Rates each student. Feeds your colleague and client feedback grade
Peer evaluation When your work together ends Every Foundry member who worked with you Rates each student. Feeds your colleague and client feedback grade
Self-assessment Before the midterm and final releases You Never part of your score. Compared with what others saw
Sprint review and demo Every two weeks You Graded as part of the sprint (Sprints)

Clients can also write to Professor Murff at any time. If a client raises a concern, faculty meets with your team that week to work through it.

The weekly deep dive

Twenty-five minutes, three questions, asked about both projects. Bring the actual work; no slides.

  1. What did each stakeholder ask for last week, and what did you deliver? Show the artifact.
  2. What will you recommend to each stakeholder this week, and why?
  3. What are you stuck on? Name anything that has blocked you for more than 48 hours.

On LinkedIn and your resume

Use your Foundry role as your title. The organization is AI Foundry, BYU Marriott School of Business.

Your role Titles you can use
Engagement Lead Engagement Lead · Senior AI Builder · Lead Forward-Deployed Engineer
Associate Associate · AI Builder · Forward-Deployed Engineer

Describe what you did and for what kind of client. Name the client only if your engagement letter allows it; otherwise describe the client by industry and size. For example:

Built and shipped an intake system for a regional health clinic, cutting incomplete intakes from 39% to 17% in two weeks. Led discovery with the clinic’s operations team and handed the system over to their staff.

The Foundry is a for-credit program, so list it as neither a paid job nor an internship, and leave LinkedIn’s employment type blank. Keep screenshots, code, data and client names private when the engagement letter makes them confidential, and ask your Project Advisor if you are not sure.

Your other commitments

Your application listed your other courses, work, projects and absences. Update it within a week of any change, and tell your team before a planned absence. Early notice lets the team plan around it.
