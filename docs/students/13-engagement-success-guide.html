Engagement Success Guide

Clients hire the Foundry with high expectations. This guide exists to help teams meet and exceed those expectations, from the first week to roll-off.

Clients have the same AI tools we do. They are looking for expertise, critical thinking and good judgment beyond what they get from AI tools and coding agents alone. Read this guide before your first client call, and refer to the checklist often.

What clients expect

Clients who hire the AI Foundry expect professional-level work.

Clients expect What it looks like
Builder expertise Working software that solves the client’s problem, built with skill across the six Builder axes.
The hours you committed 20 hours a week, the commitment for 6.0 credit hours.
Contact between calls Reply to client messages the day they arrive, ideally within minutes or hours, even if only to acknowledge them and say when the full answer is coming. If a message comes in after 10 PM, a reply the following day is appropriate. In general, overcommunicate, so the client always knows where things stand and what to expect from you next.
You’ve done your homework Read the code, documents and data the client gave you before asking questions whose answers may be in them. Keep a running log of their answers so each question is asked once.
Your own thinking and a point of view Everyone has the same starting point with AI. Your value is the critical thinking and the right questions you bring to it. Always have a point of view on the right answer, update it as new facts emerge, and be ready to share it.
A human touch Use AI for the work, then make it yours: read, check and edit everything before it goes out, emails included. Don’t send deliverables, emails or other outputs that you cannot stand behind and explain.

Your team

Each client team has three people: a Faculty Project Advisor (Professor Murff or Professor Toborg), an MBA Engagement Lead and an undergraduate Associate. Top-tier consulting firms staff engagements this way because the structure supports coaching, mentorship and growth at every level. To deliver excellent work consistently, the Foundry operates the same way.

Your Project Advisor works closely with your team: solving problems with you and reviewing deliverables before they reach the client. They meet with each team for 25 minutes in the Thursday 9:30 to 10:45 slot, meet at other times as needed, and frequently join client calls.

Role Who What they own
Faculty Project Advisor Professor Murff or Professor Toborg The client relationship: finding the client, the scope and the fee. Answers to the client for the result. Works through problems with the team, reviews deliverables, meets with the team every Thursday, and joins client calls
Engagement Lead A second-year MBA Everything an Associate does, plus: the client’s main day-to-day contact, the final say on sprint priorities when the two of you disagree, and a review of the team’s work before it goes to the Project Advisor and the client. Answers to the Project Advisor for the result
Associate An undergraduate junior or senior Owns workstreams end to end, talks with the client, and presents their own work

Because AI lets everyone build, both students build and consult: each of you owns workstreams from build to demo, talks with the client directly, and presents your own work. The Engagement Lead’s coordination is added to their build work. Both the Engagement Lead and the Associate spend 20 hours a week on the team’s work.

Leading client engagements. In a consulting firm, the engagement lead has typically been an associate first. Most Engagement Leads in the Foundry have not, so taking the lead may be a gradual transition from the Project Advisor. Early on, your Project Advisor may lead client meetings with the Engagement Lead alongside. As the Engagement Lead demonstrates they are ready, they begin to lead meetings, and the Project Advisor continues to join calls where helpful.

Two projects

Every team runs two projects at once: an outside client engagement and a Foundry product. Each has stakeholders who depend on you, and the Foundry products will have customers of their own. Treat both stakeholders as clients; everything on this page applies to each.

There are three Foundry products, and two teams work on each one. Client work rises and falls from week to week, so when one team’s client needs more of its time, the other team steps up.

The engagement, start to finish

The first week

The first week sets the pace for the engagement. Access is the most common early delay, so ask for it on day one.

Day one: ask for access, in one message. Send the client a single list of everything you need, with who on the team needs each item:

  • Code repositories, and the hosting and deployment accounts behind them
  • Data: exports, databases, or read-only reports, and who owns each
  • Documents: process maps, past attempts, and anything the client has already written down
  • The tools the team will use with them: Slack or Teams channel, shared drive, project board
  • Test accounts for any system you will build against
  • Names of the people you should talk to, beyond your main contact

Follow up on anything missing after two business days. If access is still blocked after that, raise it with your Project Advisor.

Before you touch client data, make sure you understand the client’s expectations for AI use and data security. Work with your Project Advisor to get them clear.

The kickoff call. Read everything the client has shared first. Come with an agenda, and leave with answers to these questions:

  • What does success look like at the end of the engagement?
  • Who decides, who is your day-to-day contact, and who else needs to be kept informed?
  • How will you communicate, and when is the weekly call?
  • What is the first deliverable, and when is it due?

Send the summary the same day.

Every week

  • Plan together. Plan each sprint together, across both projects.
  • Meet your Project Advisor on Thursday, and bring the actual work.
  • The client call. Send the agenda at least one business day ahead. Finish what you promised for the call, or tell the client about a slip before it. Send a summary the same day: decisions, actions, owners and dates.
  • Demo your work every week.
  • Raise risks and blockers to your Project Advisor the same day you see them.

Roll-off

When the engagement ends, the client should be able to run what you built without you:

  • Deliver a written handoff of everything you built, where it lives, and how to run it
  • Move every repository and account the client will keep into the client’s ownership
  • Return or delete client data as the engagement letter says, and give up your access

How you grow

The Consultant diamond

Your clients, your teammate and your Project Advisor rate you on the four axes of the Consultant diamond through a survey in the app, at the midpoint and at the end of each engagement. For each behavior below, they say how often you do it, from Rarely to Always.

Axis The goal The behaviors raters look for
Judgment Solve the right problem in a logical way Clarify the goal before you start. Focus on what matters most. When something isn’t working, find a better way to do it.
Communication Be understood quickly Put the main point first. Write and speak clearly, and explain technical things in plain language. When you need a decision, state it with a recommendation so it can be settled without a meeting.
Ownership Get the work done and keep people informed Do what you say you will, by the date you say. Be available when you said you would be. Keep working on a problem until it’s solved, without waiting to be told. Send updates without being asked, and report problems as soon as you find them.

Feedback every two weeks

Every two weeks, the two of you set aside 15 minutes to give each other feedback on both the Builder and the Consultant axes. The Feedback Guide shows how to run the session.
