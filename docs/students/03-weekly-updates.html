Weekly Updates

Two emails go out at the end of every week, and they do different jobs. The client update keeps the client informed and creates the record of the engagement. The Foundry update keeps the faculty advisor informed about you, individually, and is what your Weekly Update Email points are scored against.

Who sends To What it covers
Client update Client lead, once, for the team Client point of contact What the team shipped
Foundry update Every student, individually aifoundry@byu.edu What you did

Both are due Saturday by 11:59 PM.

  • First weekly email due: September 5, 2026
  • Final weekly email due: April 10, 2027
  • Total weekly emails: 28 (14 per semester)

The end-of-week timing is intentional. It forces reflection on what actually got done, sets priorities before Monday, and surfaces blockers while there is still time to fix them.

The Foundry Update

This is the individual one. Every student sends it, every week, including weeks when the client update went out under someone else’s name.

Recipients

  • To: aifoundry@byu.edu
  • CC: Your client lead

Email Format

Your update should include three sections.

1. What I worked on and accomplished this week [Required]

  • List completed tasks and deliverables
  • Be specific about outcomes, not just activities
  • Include the number, the finding, or the decision where there is one

2. Plans for next week [Required]

  • Outline your priorities and planned tasks
  • Be realistic about what you can accomplish

3. Blockers, risks, or dependencies [May exclude if none]

  • Flag anything that might impact your progress
  • Include items you need from others
  • Mention any questions or decisions needed

Close with your hours for the week, split between base and surge. This is the same number you log for utilization, and the two need to agree.

Example Email

Subject: Foundry Weekly - Ana Torres - Meridian Health - Week of Oct 5

Hi Professor Murff,

This week I worked on and accomplished the following:
- Reconciled the revenue baseline to Meridian's FY26 actuals. We were off by
  2.3% on specialty; the gap was a channel mapping issue, now corrected.
- Built the sensitivity harness for price erosion and launch timing, and ran
  the first pass across the 42 scored products.
- Ran the Wednesday client call while Tyler was out.

Next week, my priorities are:
- Finish the sensitivity pass on the remaining 18 products
- Identify which rankings are fragile and write that up for the discovery doc
- Pair with Josh on the ingestion script so I am not the only one who can run it

There are a couple things to flag:
- Still waiting on read access to the 2024-2025 gross-to-net detail (requested
  Tuesday). If it does not land next week, discovery goes out with modeled
  margin rather than actuals.
- I have a midterm Thursday and will be offline that day.

Hours: 11 base, 4 surge.

Ana

Grading

The Weekly Update Email component scores compliance only: whether your update arrived, on time.

Status Points
On time (Saturday by 11:59 PM) 2 pts
Late (by Monday 12:00 PM noon) 1 pt
After Monday noon 0 pts

Whether it was any good is graded on the Communication axis of your developmentship composite, from these same emails, by the people who had to read them. A three-line update that arrives every Saturday earns full compliance points and a poor Communication rating, and that trade is not a good one.

The Client Update

Who Sends It

The client lead sends one email on behalf of the team. Individual students do not each send their own. If the client lead is unavailable, the QC lead sends it. Missing this email is a team-level miss and shows up in the client’s evaluation of the team.

Recipients

  • To: Client point of contact
  • CC: aifoundry@byu.edu, plus all team members

Email Format

1. Accomplished this week [Required]

  • Completed work and deliverables, stated as outcomes rather than activities
  • Include the number, the finding, or the decision, not just “worked on the model”

2. Priorities for next week [Required]

  • What the team will do, realistically
  • Who owns each item

3. Decisions or input we need from you [Required, say “none” if none]

  • Anything the client owes the team
  • Any decision the team cannot make on the client’s behalf

4. Blockers, risks, or dependencies [Omit if none]

  • Anything that puts a milestone at risk
  • Flag it the week you see it, not the week it hits

Example Email

Subject: AI Foundry Weekly Update - [Client] - Week of Oct 5

Hi [Name],

Accomplished this week:
- Scored 42 of the 60 candidate products against the draft criteria. 11 clear
  the NPV multiple threshold of 3.0x under base-case assumptions.
- Reconciled the revenue baseline to your FY26 actuals. We were off by 2.3%
  on specialty; the gap was a channel mapping issue, now corrected.
- Drafted the sensitivity structure for price erosion and launch timing.

Priorities for next week:
- Finish scoring the remaining 18 products (Maria, Josh)
- Run the first full sensitivity pass and identify which rankings are fragile (Ana)
- Draft the discovery findings document for your review (Tyler)

Decisions or input we need from you:
- Confirm whether the 12% price erosion assumption should hold across all
  therapeutic areas or vary by category. This changes the ranking materially.
- We need read access to the 2024-2025 gross-to-net detail to validate margin.

Risks:
- If the gross-to-net data is not available in the next week, discovery will
  land with modeled margin rather than actuals, and we will need to revisit the
  build estimate afterward.

Thanks,
[Client lead name], on behalf of the [Client] Foundry team

What Makes an Update Good

This applies to both emails.

  • Lead with the number or the finding. “11 of 42 products clear the threshold” beats “continued portfolio scoring.”
  • Name owners. Unowned priorities do not get done.
  • Ask for what you need explicitly. Clients cannot read minds, and a blocker you did not raise is your fault, not theirs.
  • Flag risk early and unemotionally. A risk raised in week 4 is a conversation. The same risk surfaced in week 12 is a failure.
  • Be concise. Bullets, not paragraphs.
  • Never pad. A short honest week is fine. A long week of activity with no outcomes is not.

Weekly Rhythm

Day Activity
Monday Team stand-up, confirm week’s priorities and owners
Midweek Standing client call, execute
Thursday QC lead reviews anything going to the client
Friday Studio time in 1244 TNRB, cross-team critique
Saturday Client lead sends the client update; everyone sends their Foundry update, by 11:59 PM
